## Overview

wawa makes clinical software for fertility clinics in the UK, US, Canada and Australia: patient intake, treatment, scheduling, cryo storage, billing and the reporting clinics owe their regulators. I was a senior product designer in a small, flat team, working directly with clinic partners and taking features from research through to shipped frontend code.

## How I worked

### Why

wawa is dense: big tables, lots of linked data and more states than static Figma frames could keep track of.

### What I did

- Rebuilt almost the whole wawa platform from scratch in React and used it as my design tool, so every design was a working prototype with real interactions and data.
- As the team grew confident with AI, the design team, me included, moved to designing straight in the production codebase (Ruby on Rails, Phlex and Tailwind) with Claude Code.
- Built a components page in the prototype that became the groundwork for wawa's design system redesign.

### Result

A design became a pull request for engineers to review, not a file to rebuild: smaller features went from one or two days of developer time to about an hour.

## Regulatory reporting

Reporting to fertility regulators, built on data clinics already record.

### Why

- Clinics have to report every registration, cycle and outcome to their regulator (HFEA in the UK, ANZARD in Australia, BORN in Canada, SART in the US), each with its own format.
- Staff re-entered data that already existed, with no view of what was outstanding and no deadline reminders, despite penalties for late or wrong submissions.
- Many prospective clinics needed reporting before they could sign.

### What I did

- Rejected the obvious answer of reporting forms. wawa already holds the data, so reporting became verification: a dashboard of what's ready, what's wrong and what's overdue.
- Each error is fixed in place with a link back to its source record, then checked off and sent in one batch.
- One canonical data model, mapped to each regulator only at submission. Regulators without an API get the same workflow with "Export CSV" instead of "Submit".
- Deadlines and reminders for each form type, plus an activity log of who changed, checked and submitted what.
- An explorer for the regulator's own reports, with charts for timeliness, rejected records and validation errors.

### Result

The UK integration with HFEA's PRISM system went live, followed by Canada and Australia through CSV. Clinics singled out never having to log into the regulator's portal, and the Canadian regulator was keen enough on the design to look into it for themselves.

## Referrals and patient intake

One intake queue for every way a patient arrives, with AI reading the referral letters.

### Why

- Patients arrive by referral letter, web form, direct sign-up and marketing, and each new lead was mixed in with existing patients.
- Typing up a single referral took 20 to 40 minutes.
- Duplicates slipped through and referrals expired without anyone noticing.

### What I did

- Reframed referrals as one intake queue across every source, with a clear lifecycle from unprocessed to booked.
- AI extracts patient details from uploaded referral documents; staff check and correct them instead of typing.
- Duplicate detection at the point of entry, and a view of referrals about to expire.

### Result

Handling a referral went from 20–40 minutes to a few minutes; one clinic went from four referral staff to one. The extraction worked well enough that wawa planned to roll it out across all patient intake and into other parts of the platform.

## Treatment planning

wawa's first treatment planning system, from template to the patient's choice.

### Why

Three large clinics were waiting on treatment planning before they would sign, and billing and stim sheets depended on it.

### What I did

- Plans built from protocol templates (medication, tests, consents and options), with clear signs when a plan drifts from its template and a one-click reset.
- A lifecycle from suggested to sent, accepted, active and completed, with the next action always obvious.
- Settings for treatment types, protocols and templates. I explored three layouts for the same data before settling on one.
- A patient-side view to compare plans side by side on a phone and accept one.

### Result

The clinic side shipped and helped sign all three clinics. Standard templates cover 99% of cases and adapt easily for the rest, and it simplified billing and stim sheets. Clinics were asking for the patient side to make planning self-serve, and it was planned for a later phase.

## Patient health information

One health survey for every clinic, filled in by patients, checked by clinicians.

### Why

- Every clinic had its own intake survey asking the same questions in different words.
- Doctors spent appointment time asking questions and writing down answers.
- Required regulator fields were often missing because nothing captured them up front.

### What I did

- Merged every clinic's questions into one master survey that each clinic tailors, with structured answers only so the data can be reused anywhere.
- Moved collection to the patient: themed sections, one question at a time, gentler wording, and a mode that walks through only what's left.
- Answers only enter the record after a clinician reviews them, and each entry shows whether it came from the patient or the clinician.
- A builder for conditional questions, clinical labels and long lists like medications, and analytics on how long surveys take and where patients stall.

### Result

Rolled out to all clinics in the app and on the web, together with the team building the platform's data collection. Clinicians review instead of transcribe, and the missing regulator fields are now captured during intake.

## Clinical workflows

Smaller redesigns of the tools staff use all day.

### What I did

- **Stim sheets:** extending a medication took seven or more clicks and a page reload. I made it one direct action.
- **Task inbox:** clinical tasks work like an email inbox: auto-advance to the next item, keyboard shortcuts, and overdue and today filters. It became a page type reused across the platform.
- **Donors:** a dedicated donors section in cryo storage, with family limits at a glance, replacing a workflow built up from one-off fixes.
- **Cryo safety:** two-person witnessing for high-risk actions like thawing, with status for each witness.
- **Lab sheets:** instead of printing sheets to fill in by hand and type up again, a QR code on the sample opens its live digital sheet.

### Result

All of these shipped. Extending a medication went from 7+ clicks to one, saving doctors' most expensive time.

## Clinic onboarding

Self-serve setup for new clinics.

### Why

Setting up a clinic meant dozens of interdependent settings, losing your work when a dependency was missing, and days of in-person training that couldn't scale.

### What I did

- A guided checklist of setup sections, each filled in by the clinic and signed off by an approver.
- Missing dependencies, like a tax rate, can be created inside the form that needs them.
- Bulk import that matches columns, maps unknown values and catches duplicates before anyone is invited.
- A two-sided internal tool where the wawa team and the clinic share the same steps, assign them to each other and see who's working on what.

### Result

The guided setup was chosen at the company all-hands as the template for every future implementation, moving setup from days of training towards self-serve.

## Patient app redesign

A simpler patient app built around what to do now.

### Why

The existing app had grown into many separate sections. Patients mostly need to know what to do next, and some tasks, like a trigger injection, are time-critical.

### What I did

- A single timeline anchored at "now": what to do today in the middle, upcoming above, history below, and a way back to now when you scroll away.
- One detail page for every item type: medication logging, consents with several signatories, add-ons to accept or decline, appointments that go straight into your calendar.
- Overdue items are flagged calmly, not as walls of red. Every text colour meets the strictest (AAA) contrast level.
- One codebase that works as a phone app and on desktop.

### Result

It was in active development with our React Native developer when I left, heading towards an MVP.

## What I took away

- In regulated software, the better answer is usually to design a thin layer over data that already exists, not a new form asking for it again.
- Designing in code meant my designs had to survive real data and real constraints from day one.
- The same feature can differ a lot between the UK, US, Canada and Australia; designing for that from the start beats treating it as localisation later.
