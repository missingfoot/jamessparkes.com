The next thing I have been developing is the stationary. This includes things like the letterhead for corporate documents, business cards, and press releases. I went with the same grid which I designed for my previous book, the brand guidelines so that things are kept consistent. I then went ahead and started to design the actual documents themselves. Using the same baseline grid as before (4pts from margin) I was able to give the body copy enough leading to be easily readable at the size I chose and using the Gotham Light typeface chosen.

**Press Release**

I started off by creating the press release. For this I used an actual press release issued from Archos, originally just a Work document with a photo of the products, and then utilised the rules I had laid down in my guidelines and developed the overall look and feel of the document. With the top half devoted to a very large image of the product and the bottom half with the most crucial information the document was now much better balances than before.

It was also useful working with real information as this meant I had to carefully consider the placement and position of the elements on the page. Not being at to change the text in any way was a very useful restriction as this would be how it would be in real life, not being able to change something someone else had written, so I had to think carefully and plan my decisions.

**Letterhead**

For the letterhead I decided to mix things up a bit. I knew I had to have the company contact details in there somewhere, so I decided on having the tag logo at the bottom of the page on the left instead of the top right, this allowed the top of the page to be clear of anything too distracting, while also still having the logo, I could then neatly put the address and contact information next to this.

It was still import that the Archos logotype stayed at the top however as this serves as an important connection between the receiver fo the letter and the brand. They will still immediately notice the logo being at the top left where the human gaze beginning to ‘read’ things, and the next relevant thing is the content of the letter which is read next, then the information at the bottom.

**Business Cards**

For the business cards I took the concept of the colour palette to new heights by employing a ‘colour per department’ system, a ‘colour code’ you could say. From the different coloured business cards it will be easy to identify where the person works within the company if the department description is cryptic to someone unprivy of such matters. I also wanted to keep the business cards minimal with only the most relevant information, such as their name, department, what they do and their contact information. This is accompanied by a black letterform logo and logotype. This all take place on the reverse, which is white, and also leaves a lot of white space, useful for any notes the receiver may need to inscribe upon it’s surface using a pen or pencil of some description. This makes it extremely practical.

The front of the business card is the chosen colour, for example, Teal. The teal colour is accompanied by only a negative letterform logo, which is clean and doesn’t revel too much without being cryptic, it prompts the receiver to turn it over to reveal the information they were in pursuit of.
