For the Archos rebrand I have also been looking at implementing some examples of an advertising campaign. I have taken a technical aspect of one of their flagship products, the Platinum range of tablets, which feature a very high resolution IPS display. So taking this technical aspect I need to relate this to the brand principles in order to come up with a theme. I looked at the aspirational concept I had come up with earlier in the brand value stages.

I then was looking back at my previous ideas and convepts and wa sdrawn to the idea of using space as a back drop. Space and nebulae are inspirational,  aspirational, big and beautiful space scapes. This will do rather well I thought to myself. I started to develop this further and took away the black and white style from before an replaced it by overlaying the Archos brand colours over the image of the Carina nebula which I had picked out to be the perfect representation.

I then started thinking of the way people use and hold tablets. I was holding my hands up in front of me, when suddenly it hit me. I could use the tablet as a sort of window, and things on tablets scroll fluidly so this concept of the nebula extending past the confides of the actual tablet was realised. This was the perfect theme and named it the Carina Campaign. With these two simple elements, the nebula imagery and the scrolling window ‘looking out into the cosmos’ was what will become the fundamental building blocks of the advertising campaign.

I then focused on producing two examples of advertising materials, two 48 sheet posters and two magazine advert variations. The 48 sheet posters were to show a normal campaign which siomply focused on creating awareness of the new flagship range, and the idea was the elaborate on this with individual Carina Campaign posters feature each product and it’s individual colours on the poster. This will act as a contrast to draw the audience into the campaign.

The magazine campaigns are to show how the form factor will work in a small advert space and also to show what they would look like in a magazine environment. This was particularly useful to me as it is good to see the materials in some kind of context to see if they work and feel that they did very well.
