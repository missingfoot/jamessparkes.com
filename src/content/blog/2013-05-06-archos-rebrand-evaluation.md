Looking back at my original objectives and the goals I initially wanted to achieve  I need to take into consideration the evolution of the project, which I think was a very good think, a very helpful process and one that I learnt a great deal from:

> “The primary goal is to create a portfolio piece that shows a wide range of skills in various areas such as traditional graphic design theory and practice, but also digital design fields like photo manipulation, web and product design. It will also be useful to explore the process of taking a product through all the stages of development and then using the company as a vehicle to sell to an audience.
> 
> I would like to have experience in as many things as possible to avoid industry irrelevance so that I can always adapt to the changing markets. I also posses interest in the subject area, having always wanted to do something in the technology industry, I feel undertaking a major project like this will provide ample excuse to test my abilities in design.”

It became clear that some of these were in the grander scheme of things, unnecessary, so I decided to trim back some of the more unnecessarily ambitions such as web and product design. It became apparently that these were rather inessential skills to be developing in the greater context of the project, which was to rebrand Archos. This could be effectively done through print-based design alone and the very notion of product design seems frankly, quite absurd.

In hindsight, I don’t think spending time on developing new products would have been time well spent. As Mary had said at the start, I’d be using up resources and time, and she was correct. I went on to think of solutions to this, I knew I could find resources somewhere to take care of the products, I would simply acquired stock tablets in various angles to be used in advertising campaigns and for general shots of the products, and photographs for the packaging mockups. These were much more manageable that my previous ambitions to create product line-ups and so on.

The process of developing the materials for this project has been exceedingly helpful to me as I have had to think about the entire rebrand from start to finish all the while maintaining the initial goals I had set. For instance the brand needed to come across as higher quality but also pushing the companies aspirational and innovative history in tablets and I also wanted to make them look ‘friendly’ so they appeared more approachable to their audience. This was achieved by taking the colour palette and muting the tones so they felt softer and calmer, and the use of the rounded typeface in advertising material furthered this narrative.

In conclusion I feel content with the overall completed project. It was an interesting experience to go through something of that scale and seeing it evolve slightly from the beginning, mostly for the better. Some pitfalls I encountered included the annual report, which was a bit more of a task than I imagined it would be. Lacking any useful data and any official example of an annual report from Archos themselves it was difficult to compile information in a way that made sense in context to the company.

I had hoped to do something more interesting, perhaps infographics with real data but data from other sources was too hard to place alongside body copy itself also from other sources. The book felt lacking in any real narrative when I simply placed things without ample reason, so I had no choice but to take a couple of reports from another companies and replicate what those had done.

This could have been combated by thoroughly researching annual reports and spending more time compiling information myself at a much earlier stage. This would have allowed me to bring in elements I had seen in many sources of inspiration when looking at well design annual reports on Behance and such.

Besides some setbacks like that, I believe I have accomplished most of what I set out to do and as a result have some good portfolio pieces I can be proud to show. The overall process has also been very insightful to my process of working, I realise that I very much need deadlines inorder to get things done. Another thing I will be taking away from this project is that I need to know how and when to set myself my own deadlines, as these would have been extremely useful at an earlier process.

This project, perhaps more than others, has given me a good idea of the scale of things that can come up later on in the real world and has shown that I need to think of things slightly differently to what I once did. I have had to push myself in ways unparalleled before, and because of that my experiences and what I have learn have been much richer, I had to find solutions to thing I had never needed to think of before and had to overcome problems, and have experiences the evolution large projects like this take on. I would certainly like to think I have a better understanding of things now than when I started, of which is leading me to become a better designer as I continue to develop my knowledge and skills.
