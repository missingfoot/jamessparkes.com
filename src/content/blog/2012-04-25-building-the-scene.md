As I begun chipping away at the surface of my piece it became more evident how much time this was going to take. I knew that the resolution of the A3 would show off the slightest flaws which meant I would need to work at a level of detail I have never done before. I noticed this as soon as I started gathering more resources such as images of trees. Everything would need to look good even when zoomed in at 100% so it was rather daunting as I zoomed out to view the entire image that I had only done a small area.

I began by taking chipped, cracked and weathered concrete textures and transforming them to conform to the shapes and curves of the roads using the transform controls such as shearing and warp. This allowed me to mould it roughly into shape to get the correct perspective relative to the environment. As the canvas was so huge I also need extremely huge images for the textures as the quality degrades considerably when the perspective is changed from looking down onto it to looking at the side. This meant I needed to download the very high resolution and tiled version of textures from CG Textures.

Another thing I began working on today was the trees. This was especially troublesome as I’d never done trees before. At first it seemed like it would be a simple case of using the refine edge tool, however this still wasn’t perfect and I decided to improve the background removal by having a duplicated layer with the multiply blend mode on the bottom one, and an inverted mask for the normal layer, and I then coloured in the normal layer, revealing only parts of the tree that I wanted the full opacity of, leaving the edges the correct colour so there was no halo or any colour contaminated coming through the many leaves.

When I did this to a few trees however I cam across a problem when looking at the scene zoomed out. The trees I had painstakingly cut out and placed looked very unnatural due to the background removal still not being perfect. They looked out of place especially as the edges had a strange look to them from the multiply layer underneath. It occurred to me that I couldn’t simply refine edge the tree’s leaves but had to use the pen tool to cut away the background perfectly. This was in fact completely impossible in the time I had given myself to complete the project, so it was back to CG Textures to download their pre-alpha trees that had already had that treatment done to them.

These trees where almost perfectly cut out and it required only some extra pen tool to cut out a few remaining bits of background of each tree. It was then a case of blending each tree into the environment so that it belonged. I then needed to colour correct the trees so they all appeared to belong together as they were different colours and shades making them look odd when placed so close together. Not having to worry too much about the removal of the background for each tree meant I had more time to think about their placement and warping their shape to conform to where I was placing them.

The technique I had tried with the trees however did work very well on the ivy and vines I begun placing along the edges of the overpass. There was a lot less leaves to go around with the Wacom pen so it was not as time consuming as an entire tree and parts of the vines could be cloned as to create less work for myself in their background removal and to also build up shapes that I could warm around the curvature of the walls. After I had built up a good shape of vines I could then clean them up with some layer masks and painting around them with to take away any stray colour contamination. I then colour corrected them so they looked like they belonged in the scene.
