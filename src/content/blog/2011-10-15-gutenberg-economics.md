**Gutenberg Economics**

Is some knowledge more powerful than others?

Knowledge is Power

> “Printing, gunpowder and the compass: These three have changed the whole face and state of things throughout the world; the first in literature, the second in warfare, the third in navigation; whence have followed innumerable changes, in so much that no empire, no sect, no star seems to have exerted greater power and influence in human affairs than these mechanical discoveries.”

*Francis Bacon, 1620*

**Gunpowder**  
Professional armies, castles made obsolete, allowed indigenous populations to be easily beaten, allowed naval warfare, required taxation, made normal citizens weaker in comparison

**The Compass**  
Navigation, discovery of new lands, growing awareness of the world, international trade, naval trade, naval warfare, increased profits for people

**The Printing Press**  
The spread of knowledge, cheap books, loss of control by the church, mass communication, anonymous communication, newspapers, political pamphlets, literature, revolution, democracy

*Gunpowder = Military Might*  
*Compass = Exploration*  
*Printing Press = Communication*

**Johnannes Gutenberg**

In the mid-15th century Johannes Gutenberg invented a mechanical way of making books, which is considered to be the first example of mass production. Before Gutenberg every book had to be copied by hand by monks.

It allowed the process to be sped up without sacrificing quality. Gutenberg printed a Bible which sold fantastically, news of the bible reached the church and the Church used the printing press from this point on.

The printing press was a technology that helped spread the word of God. It allowed opinions and knowledge to travel and persuade.

*   Gutenberg’s invention laid the foundation for the commercial mass production of books
*   Books soon became cheaper, more widely available
*   People could follow debates and take part in discussions of matters that concerned them
*   It allowed discussion as well as dissemination
*   Printing presses were seized and carefully monitored
*   The Church and Government controlled and regulated their output

The Church subsequently controlled access to information and persuaded people how the world works and also generated income.

**Martin Luther**

In 1517 Martin Luther posted Ninety-Five Theses on the Wittenberg Church Door. Theses 86: “Why does not the Pope, whose wealth is today greater than the wealth of the richest Crassus, build this one basilica of St. Peter with his own money rather than with the money of poor believers?”

Martin Luther thought in order to be saved, you had to leave it to Christ, and you had to simply cling to what Christ had done for you. He was a rebel activist, and although he was well connected he was an individual that lived in one region. He could have been stopped, perhaps by force, but ideas travel and can’t be stopped, nor can a printed page.

*Luther = Heat Magazine*

Luther went mainstream, producing entertaining junk. More prolific, more scandalous, and he used the language or dialect spoken by the ordinary people. In effect Luther created a counterculture press.

**Lutherian Impacts**

Alongside the anti-Church publishing, Luther had the Bible translated into German. He wanted to show people that his reading of the bible was correct.

*   Other people disagreed with Luther’s interpretation thus faith became subjective
*   Luther’s subversive use of the press exposed flaws with the things that the Catholic Church was persuading people were true (challenging their rhetoric)
*   The Church broke into various divisions that interpreted the Bible differently, each with their own rhetorics and ideologies

**After Luther**

*   Erosion of absolute power of Catholic Church
*   Promoted interpretation of the Bible
*   Encouraged literacy in the general population
*   Helped create an academic community that challenged and shared ideas

**Before Modernism**

The Quarrel of the Ancients & Moderns argued that the Moderns (peopling living in the 18th century) were morally and artistically superior to the Ancients (the Greeks and Romans). Conservatives (people in power religiously or royalty) tended to support the arguments of the Ancients, while Progressives sided with the Moderns.

**Before Modernism: The Age of Enlightenment**

These ideas led to a focus on human achievement, i.e., look at what we can do and a further idea of the power of mankind and the mind of reason. In the 18th century, the Age of Enlightenment freed thinkers from superstition and ignorance and led to a scientific revolution.

*   Galileo – Earth and planets revolve around a stationary Sun at the centre of the solar system
*   Newton – Mathematical descriptions of the laws of mechanics and gravitation

**Before Modernism: The Notion of Truth**

Galileo and Newton’s ideas undermined the Church and the other conservatives that supported the Ancients. This led to a notion that truth could be discovered through reason and that truth could be discovered by individuals applying science.

By the mid 18th century progressive thinkers thought that reason could be applied to anything, improving things as a result such as politics, health and social conditions.

**Before Modernism: Improving Society & Freedom**

A new and better society could be created by man. The truth shall set you free from the shackles of corrupt institutions such as the Church & monarchy. Freedom become a key concept, through truth and freedom society will be improved.

**Before Modernism: Equality**

In 1763, Jean-Jacques Rousseau declared the right of liberty and equality for all men. In 1776 the Declaration of Independence of the newly founded United States used phrases such as: “we hold these truths to be self-evident” and which underpins the notion that “all men are created equal”.

Before you can agree with another person or group or feel connected to them in any meaningful way, you need to be able to share information and exchange ideas. In a very real sense, the American revolution could not have happened without the mail and the other systems by which Americans exchanged ideas.

Building consensus and a communal identity required a shared understanding that could only be developed through an ongoing civic conversation that took place through the use of informal conversations, letters, speeches, meetings, newspapers, pamphlets, broadsides, and books.

But the Printing Press didn’t only have a positive impact on society. As a technology it had no bias, it simply communicated ideas, good or bad.

**Gutenberg Economics**

The Gutenberg press and the technologies that followed it allowed a smaller number of voices to be communicated to a mass of listeners; ‘one to many’ communication or ‘producer to audience’.
