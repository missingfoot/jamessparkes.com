I have chosen to go with the BBC brief from D&AD as I feel this would allow me to exercise some user interface design skills that I think need brushing up on. I used to do quite a bit of web design but have recently not put much focus on it, I would like to change that by branching out and making an attempt at this brief.

As with any D&AD brief I have been looking through it very carefully as they are often rather cryptic and ambiguous in terms of what they actually want. By sifting through and picking out various key phrases and terminology this is helping me build a picture of what I should begin to do to further the project along. I’m already considering what aspects I’d like to cover in my own answer to the brief and have watched the video on the website which was helpful.

The brief insinuates that I can pick a particular aspect of the BBC to cover for this brief and since the most used part which I use personally is the BBC iPlayer I plan on doing something with that. Essentially what they are asking for is ideas on how to make the BBC online presence more ‘live’, dynamically updated and relevant to the present.

For some inspiration I looked at a video which was recommended to me from the Guardian about all the different ways that news and events permeate our lives and all the outlets they utilise to be constantly tapped into our collective consciousness wherever we are thanks to digital communications technology.

I have also been looking at the various directions I could take my idea. I would need to consider things like smartphones and tablets as well as the website. Apps are being used by content distributors like the BBC to facilitate all kinds of interactivity with their audience and the various programmes they make such as the Formula 1 live lap and positions app and the interactive Antiques Roadshow app. Things like this were also prevalent during the Olympics with many ways of accessing the plethora of data being generated on an hourly basis for people to consume via any medium almost.

Other things I have gleaned from some research are that I will need to think about the places people might be viewing the content. According to various articles [such as this one from the Guardian](http://www.guardian.co.uk/media/2012/jun/11/nation-of-audience-participation) many people are consuming television while using a laptop or a tablet, and often have a smartphone while using either as well. It will be important to consider a seamless transition between each medium.

> 60% of UK viewers now have a laptop or tablet computer running while they are watching television and are very often commenting on the show unfolding
> 
> There has been a big consumer uptake of tablets and iPhones; we started noticing people talking about shows while watching them
> 
> Tablets and laptops will become the way that people watch television in the future … The box in the corner will become the second screen
